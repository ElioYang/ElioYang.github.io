How Autonomous Is AI in Science?

An inventory of 1,743 AI-in-science records, and a text classifier that predicts the autonomy level each project has been labeled with.

Inventory with Prof. Anita Nikolich · Classifier: James Scholar project, advised by Prof. Nigel Bosch · Ongoing

Question

As AI systems take over more of the research process, how much of the decision-making still passes through a person, and where along the workflow does it stop?

What I did

  • With Prof. Anita Nikolich, built an inventory of AI systems, tools, and studies across scientific disciplines, coded by research-workflow stage and AI capability level.
  • Verified entries against primary sources (papers, institutional releases, award pages), down to the exact model versions each system uses, and removed systems with no actual AI component.
  • For my James Scholar honors project (advised by Prof. Nigel Bosch), selected 320 projects from the inventory and trained a classifier that predicts each project's labeled autonomy level from its text description, using word and bigram TF–IDF features and an RBF-kernel support vector machine.
  • Developed the model on 256 projects and held out the other 64 as a test set.
  • The inventory is the groundwork for a threat model of security risks in AI-driven science.

Result

On the 64 held-out projects, the model reached a macro-F1 of 0.645 and an accuracy of 0.719.

This measures agreement with the existing autonomy labels, which were not independently re-verified in this study. It does not yet show that the model captures how autonomous a system really is. An independent test on newly added projects is planned; 14 new projects of the same type are available so far.
