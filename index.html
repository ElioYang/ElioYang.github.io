Research portfolio

Yuxing Yang

I study how defaults and access rules decide things for people without their knowledge, and how to make those decisions visible and contestable.

I am an undergraduate at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, studying Information Science and Data Science in the School of Information Sciences.

Many of the most consequential decisions a system makes about a person are made before that person arrives. A twelve-year-old inherits the privacy defaults of an adult. A teenager's settings stay in a parent's hands after the reason for that has passed. A reader cannot tell which library record an AI-rewritten passage came from.

I use Contextual Integrity to locate where those decisions break with people's expectations. Then I build prototypes and measurement testbeds to check whether a proposed fix actually works, and what it quietly leaves in place.

Projects

01

Who Sets the Default?

Laws now require "high privacy by default" for children. I study what those defaults should be at each age, and whether young users can see and change them.

Sole-authored papers (SOTICS 2026, PrivaCI 2026), with feedback from Prof. Madelyn Sanfilippo · Default Inspector demo

↗02

Designing Childhood Online

Child online-safety laws are design specifications written by people who are not designers. I code what harm they think they are preventing, and how that idea of harm becomes interface rules.

Undergraduate research assistant · with Dr. Soumita Mukherjee and Prof. Madelyn Sanfilippo, UIUC School of Information Sciences · 2026–present

Work in progress. Findings are preliminary.
↗03

When Access Should Change

When a person's role changes, what they can see and who can see them should change too, and someone should be able to show that it did.

Sole author · Under review, ACM CSLAW 2027

↗04

Tracing AI-Transformed Text

When an AI rewrites a library text, can we trace it back to its source, and what signal are we really following?

Sole author · Mentored by Dr. Teddy Roland · JCDL 2026, ASIS&T 2026

↗05

How Autonomous Is AI in Science?

An inventory of 1,743 AI-in-science records, and a text classifier that predicts the autonomy level each project has been labeled with.

Inventory with Prof. Anita Nikolich · Classifier: James Scholar project, advised by Prof. Nigel Bosch · Ongoing

↗06

Measuring the Cost of Post-Quantum Migration

A testbed I built to measure what post-quantum key exchange actually costs on telemetry links, starting with a bias I found in my own first version. First-place undergraduate paper, ITC 2026.

Lead author · Faculty advisor: Dominic Saebeler · Two papers, ITC 2026
